Automation & ELV

Building systems that communicate

Plan the low-current and control systems around the building, its users and its other trades. Coordination, clear interfaces and a considered handover are as important as the equipment itself.

01 · Building automation & ELV

Building Management System (BMS)

A Building Management System (BMS), also known as a Building Automation System (BAS), can monitor and control selected building systems such as HVAC, lighting, power, fire-safety interfaces and security. Domicile lists BMS design, installation and integration among its services. The points, control authority and interfaces must follow the approved project design and applicable life-safety requirements.

Typical scope

  • Review control drawings, equipment schedules and integration points.
  • Coordinate control panels, sensors, actuators and communication networks.
  • Confirm protocol compatibility, such as BACnet or Modbus, for specified interfaces.
  • Test agreed points and provide operator handover information.

Why consider it

  • Provides a shared view of connected plant status.
  • Helps operators identify alarms and operating conditions.
  • Supports scheduled control and review of available trend data.
  • Can coordinate selected systems where interfaces are compatible.

Typical applications

  • Commercial offices and mixed-use buildings.
  • Residential towers and shared facilities.
  • Hotels, schools and other multi-system properties.
  • Existing sites requiring upgrades or controls maintenance.

Delivery focus: confirm the points list and interfaces first, coordinate the approved control intent, then test and document the agreed functions.

02 · Building automation & ELV

Structured Cabling

Structured cabling provides an organised passive network infrastructure for data and other compatible low-voltage services. Outlet positions, cable categories, containment, pathways and test requirements should follow the project design.

Typical scope

  • Review outlet schedules, routes, rack layouts and specifications.
  • Coordinate containment, cable routes, outlets and termination points.
  • Label and organise agreed links at outlets and cabinets.
  • Test links to the project requirements and record results.

Why consider it

  • Creates an orderly and traceable cabling layout.
  • Supports moves, adds and changes when properly documented.
  • Helps simplify troubleshooting and link identification.
  • Allows network endpoints to be planned with the fit-out.

Typical applications

  • Office floors and commercial fit-outs.
  • Residential common areas and service rooms.
  • Retail, education and hospitality spaces.
  • New network outlets or structured recabling works.

Delivery focus: coordinate routes and outlet locations before installation; agree labelling and test documentation with the project team.

03 · Building automation & ELV

Access Control System

Electronic access control manages entry at designated doors and controlled areas. Door hardware, life-safety requirements, user permissions and interfaces must be coordinated with the building's approved design and operating policy.

Typical scope

  • Review door schedules, access levels and specified hardware.
  • Coordinate readers, controllers, locking devices and door contacts.
  • Configure agreed access groups and operator functions.
  • Test door operation and agreed integrations with relevant parties.

Why consider it

  • Applies different permissions to defined areas.
  • Provides an administrative record of system events where supported.
  • Supports controlled credentials instead of unmanaged key distribution.
  • Can connect to other systems when interfaces are designed and approved.

Typical applications

  • Office entrances, meeting areas and server rooms.
  • Residential building entrances and staff-only areas.
  • Retail back-of-house and stock areas.
  • Facilities with defined access zones and operator roles.

Delivery focus: coordinate fail-safe or fail-secure requirements, escape routes and door hardware with the responsible design and life-safety teams.

04 · Building automation & ELV

Intruder Alarm System

An intruder alarm uses the specified detection and notification devices to report defined events at protected areas. Coverage, arming arrangements and response procedures should be set by the client's security requirements and project design.

Typical scope

  • Review protected zones, device locations and user requirements.
  • Coordinate control equipment, detection devices and notification points.
  • Set up agreed zones and operator functions.
  • Test specified devices and explain normal system operation.

Why consider it

  • Provides alerts for configured events in defined zones.
  • Helps establish a consistent arming and disarming process.
  • Supports review of system events where the selected system provides records.
  • Can be planned around working hours and area use.

Typical applications

  • Offices and restricted work areas.
  • Retail units and storage spaces.
  • Residential service and shared areas.
  • Existing systems requiring extension or maintenance.

Delivery focus: agree zone coverage, user responsibilities and any monitoring interfaces before installation and testing.

05 · Building automation & ELV

Public Address System

A public address system distributes live or recorded audio to designated zones. Speaker selection, audibility, zoning and any emergency-voice functionality must match the approved acoustic, architectural and life-safety requirements.

Typical scope

  • Review zoning, intended messages and coverage requirements.
  • Coordinate speakers, cabling, amplifiers and operator equipment.
  • Configure agreed zones and source selection.
  • Test operation and document the agreed system functions.

Why consider it

  • Allows messages to be directed to selected areas.
  • Supports routine announcements and background audio as specified.
  • Can be arranged for different building uses and operating zones.
  • Provides a defined platform for authorised operators to make announcements.

Typical applications

  • Reception areas, corridors and shared spaces.
  • Commercial, retail and hospitality environments.
  • Schools and other public-facing facilities.
  • Buildings with zoned announcement requirements.

Delivery focus: distinguish general PA from any certified emergency voice system; applicable approval and testing requirements must be confirmed for the project.

06 · Building automation & ELV

ELV System AMC

An annual maintenance contract (AMC) can define planned visits, inspection activities and call-out arrangements for listed ELV systems. The included equipment, response terms, exclusions and any replacement parts should be agreed in writing before the contract starts.

Typical scope

  • Record covered systems, equipment and site contacts.
  • Agree a preventive visit schedule and inspection checklist.
  • Inspect accessible equipment and report observed issues.
  • Define help-desk, call-out, repair and parts terms in the proposal.

Why consider it

  • Sets out maintenance responsibilities and visit expectations.
  • Creates a record of reported faults and completed service visits.
  • Helps plan routine checks for covered equipment.
  • Makes exclusions and additional work easier to identify.

Typical applications

  • Occupied commercial and residential buildings.
  • Sites with multiple ELV systems and equipment rooms.
  • Properties moving from project handover to operations.
  • Existing installations needing a defined service arrangement.

Delivery focus: the final service frequency, response times and inclusions are contract-specific; confirm these in the written quotation.

Smart building solutions

Make building controls work together

Smart-building work should start with the owner's priorities, existing systems and the level of control people actually need. Compatible interfaces and clear user expectations shape the solution.

07 · Smart building solutions

Smart Solutions

Smart-building solutions can connect selected controls, sensors and user interfaces to simplify everyday operation. There is no single package that fits every building: functions, supported devices, privacy considerations and integrations are established during scope definition.

Typical scope

  • Identify user needs, existing equipment and desired control points.
  • Assess the stated compatibility and interfaces of proposed devices.
  • Coordinate selected controls, sensors and user-facing functions.
  • Configure agreed scenes or routines and explain their use.

Why consider it

  • Brings selected everyday controls into a clearer user experience.
  • Can coordinate compatible functions across specified spaces.
  • Allows operating routines to be considered during design.
  • Can be phased around site constraints and project priorities.

Typical applications

  • Homes and apartments with defined automation needs.
  • Meeting rooms and small commercial suites.
  • Hospitality and shared amenity spaces.
  • Upgrades that need to work with selected existing equipment.

Delivery focus: agree the functions, supported devices, network dependencies and user access arrangements before confirming the final design.

08 · Smart building solutions

Lighting Control System

Lighting control organises how selected lights are switched, dimmed or grouped. The control method, fixture and driver compatibility, intended scenes and local override needs should be resolved with the lighting design.

Typical scope

  • Review lighting plans, fixture schedules and control intent.
  • Coordinate control devices, interfaces, circuits and local switches.
  • Configure the agreed zones, schedules or scenes.
  • Test operation with the specified luminaires and hand over settings.

Why consider it

  • Matches lighting operation to different tasks and spaces.
  • Provides a consistent way to recall agreed lighting scenes.
  • Can support schedules and sensor-based control where specified.
  • Helps coordinate user controls with the completed interior layout.

Typical applications

  • Homes, apartments and residential common areas.
  • Offices, boardrooms and meeting rooms.
  • Hotels, retail spaces and reception areas.
  • Refurbishments requiring new lighting-control zones.

Delivery focus: verify load and driver compatibility and agree scene requirements with the lighting designer and end user.

MEP Services

MEP services cover project-defined mechanical, electrical and plumbing works. The specific disciplines, equipment, capacities, materials and testing requirements vary by project and are confirmed against its drawings and specifications.

Typical scope

  • Review issued drawings, schedules, specifications and site constraints.
  • Coordinate agreed services with other trades and the construction sequence.
  • Install the included equipment, distribution and connections.
  • Carry out applicable checks and support the specified handover.

Why consider it

  • Coordinates building services with the wider construction package.
  • Clarifies scope interfaces, access and sequencing before work starts.
  • Supports planned inspection and testing against project requirements.
  • Creates a defined route for resolving drawing and site queries.

Typical applications

  • New commercial and residential construction.
  • Tenant fit-outs and building refurbishment.
  • Selected services modifications or extensions.
  • Projects requiring coordinated specialist installation works.

Delivery focus: confirm which MEP disciplines and quantities are included in the quotation; approvals, utility connections and specialist design responsibilities must be agreed separately where relevant.

10 · MEP & power infrastructure

Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS)

A UPS provides backup power to selected connected loads during specified supply interruptions or disturbances. Required runtime, load profile, redundancy, battery arrangement, room conditions and bypass provisions determine the appropriate design and must be verified.

Typical scope

  • Review the protected-load schedule and required autonomy.
  • Coordinate the UPS, batteries, distribution and monitoring interfaces.
  • Confirm installation and service-access requirements for the space.
  • Test agreed operating modes and document the installed arrangement.

Why consider it

  • Supports selected equipment during defined power events.
  • Provides a planned power-protection arrangement for critical loads.
  • Allows battery inspection and replacement planning.
  • Can offer monitoring when compatible interfaces are included.

Typical applications

  • Network racks and communications equipment.
  • Control, security and building-management equipment.
  • Selected office, retail and operational loads.
  • Sites requiring backup to specified sensitive equipment.

Delivery focus: final sizing and expected runtime depend on measured or specified loads, battery condition, site conditions and the agreed design assumptions.

Finishes & general maintenance

Complete the space and care for it over time

Finishes and maintenance need to account for existing conditions, access, working hours and the client's selected materials. A site survey helps define the practical scope before a proposal is prepared.

11 · Finishes & general maintenance

Gypsum & Painting

Gypsum and painting works help create or refresh interior surfaces. The finish, substrate preparation, access panels, service coordination and paint system should be agreed against the space's use and project finish schedule.

Typical scope

  • Inspect the area and identify existing surface conditions.
  • Agree partitions, ceilings, access provisions and finish requirements.
  • Prepare and apply the specified paint or surface finish.
  • Coordinate protection, access and clean completion with the site team.

Why consider it

  • Supports interior alterations and refurbishment work.
  • Provides a defined finish schedule for review and approval.
  • Allows service access to be planned with ceiling and wall details.
  • Helps separate preparation, materials and finish expectations.

Typical applications

  • Office and retail fit-outs.
  • Residential room alterations and repainting.
  • Ceiling repairs and interior refurbishment.
  • Common-area refreshes and fit-out reinstatement.

Delivery focus: confirm measured quantities, access, substrate repairs and sample or colour approval before ordering finishes.

12 · Finishes & general maintenance

Floor Coating

A floor-coating system is selected for the intended use and the condition of the substrate. Preparation, moisture, curing time, chemical exposure, traffic and slip-resistance requirements all affect the specification and the installation sequence.

Typical scope

  • Inspect the surface, use conditions and access limitations.
  • Agree the coating system, preparation and finish requirements.
  • Prepare the substrate and apply the specified coating layers.
  • Observe product curing requirements and review the completed area.

Why consider it

  • Creates a specified, continuous finish for selected floor areas.
  • Offers a system choice based on the intended site use.
  • Allows maintenance and cleaning expectations to be discussed up front.
  • Helps plan work zones and access during installation and curing.

Typical applications

  • Service rooms and selected back-of-house spaces.
  • Workshops, storage areas and utility floors.
  • Parking or plant areas where the chosen system is suitable.
  • Existing floors included in a refurbishment package.

Delivery focus: a site assessment is important; performance depends on the specified product, preparation quality, environment and the actual service conditions.

13 · Finishes & general maintenance

General Maintenance

General maintenance covers agreed building repair and upkeep tasks. The work is defined by the issue, site access, affected system and any specialist or authority requirements; a description and photographs help with initial assessment.

Typical scope

  • Record the reported issue, location and available site information.
  • Inspect the affected area where a survey is arranged.
  • Submit the agreed repair scope, materials and access needs.
  • Complete authorised work and report any excluded or follow-up items.

Why consider it

  • Provides a clear point of contact for agreed repair work.
  • Helps distinguish inspection, repair and replacement tasks.
  • Allows priorities and access windows to be planned.
  • Documents the work completed and any recommended next steps.

Typical applications

  • Residential and commercial properties.
  • Common areas, offices and tenant spaces.
  • Small repairs and planned refurbishment items.
  • Follow-up works identified during a site inspection.

Delivery focus: availability, response time, included trades and materials depend on the agreed quotation or maintenance contract.
